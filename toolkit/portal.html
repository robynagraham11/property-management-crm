Quick Start

Two checklists: one to make sure you may legally operate, and one for every new client file. Run both before touching any tenancy.

1 · Am I legal to operate today?

Complete the compliance foundation — see 00-compliance-foundation.md.

  1. Stonehaus Realty Corp. brokerage licence authorizes rental property management — confirmed in writing.
  2. My own licence under the brokerage covers RPM services — confirmed with BCFSA.
  3. City or Township of Langley business licence requirements confirmed (me + each property).
  4. I know the brokerage trust account rules and where every rent and deposit dollar goes.
  5. E&O insurance covers property management work.
  6. Current BCFSA disclosure forms and RTB forms downloaded.

2 · New client file checklist

Run this for every new owner and every new tenancy.

  1. BCFSA disclosure given to owner before any service.
  2. Service model chosen (coordinated / full-service / placement-only) and agreement signed.
  3. Strata units: Form B + bylaws reviewed; rental restrictions confirmed.
  4. Municipal rental business licence confirmed for the property.
  5. Applicant gets tenant disclosure before showings or applications.
  6. RTB-1 + addenda signed; deposits (max ½ month + ½ month pet) to trust.
  7. RTB-27 move-in inspection done and given to tenant.
  8. Form K signed (strata) and notice of owner's name/address served.

Category 4

Inspections

Condition reports protect the deposit. Entry notices protect the tenant's quiet enjoyment. Both are non-negotiable habits.

RTB form — official

RTB-27 Condition Inspection Report (Move-In)

Records the unit's condition at the start of the tenancy. Without it, the landlord loses the right to claim against the security or pet damage deposit.

When to use
Move-in — offer the tenant a chance to participate.
Who signs
Owner/agent + tenant (note it if tenant declines).
RTB forms page ↗
RTB form — official

RTB-27 Condition Inspection Report (Move-Out)

Compares the unit's condition against the move-in report. It is the evidence that supports any deposit deduction for damage beyond wear and tear.

When to use
Move-out — offer participation again; do the report even if the tenant skips it.
Who signs
Owner/agent + tenant (if present).
RTB forms page ↗
Template — RTA s. 29

Notice of Entry

Written notice of at least 24 hours, with date, time window (8 a.m.–9 p.m.) and a lawful reason. No official form exists — use the drafted template and keep a copy.

When to use
Inspections, repairs, showings. Emergencies need no notice.
Who signs
You, on behalf of the owner.

Category 5

Notices

Every notice that changes a tenancy has an official form and a strict clock. Serve the current official version, count the days carefully, and keep proof of service.

RTB-7 — official

Notice of Rent Increase

Rent can only increase once every 12 months, with 3 full months' written notice, at or below the annual provincial cap. Serve the official form only.

When to use
Any lawful rent increase.
Who signs
Owner/agent.
Check current cap ↗ Form ↗
RTB-30 — official

10 Day Notice to End Tenancy (Unpaid Rent or Utilities)

For unpaid rent or utilities. The tenant has 5 days to pay and cancel the notice. If unpaid, proceed via a Direct Request (RTB-46 + RTB-12L-DR) or an RTB dispute application.

When to use
Rent/utilities unpaid past the due date.
Who signs
Owner/agent.
RTB forms page ↗
RTB-33 — official

1 Month Notice to End Tenancy (Cause or Tenant Notice)

One combined form: the landlord uses it to end a tenancy for cause (repeated late payment, serious interference, unlawful activity), and the tenant uses it to give notice to move out.

When to use
Landlord: documented RTA cause. Tenant: one full month, effective the day before rent is due.
Who signs
Whichever party gives notice.
RTB forms page ↗
RTB-32L — official

3 Month Notice (Landlord's Use of Property)

Ends the tenancy so the owner or a close family member can live there, or after a sale where the buyer will occupy. Now a 3-month notice generated through the RTB web portal (not a downloadable form — changed July 18, 2024). Compensation of one month's rent is generally owed.

When to use
Genuine owner/family/buyer occupation only.
Who signs
Owner/agent, via the RTB portal (tenancydispute.gov.bc.ca → Landlord Use).
RTB portal ↗ Eviction rules ↗
RTB-29 — official

4 Month Notice (Demolition or Conversion)

Ends the tenancy to demolish, extensively renovate, or convert the unit. Longer notice, with compensation rules — get this one right or it fails.

When to use
Major renovation, demolition, conversion.
Who signs
Owner/agent.
RTB forms page ↗
RTB-8 — official

Mutual Agreement to End a Tenancy

A signed agreement when both sides agree to end the tenancy early. Safer than a verbal arrangement — it can prevent a later dispute.

When to use
Any agreed early end to a tenancy.
Who signs
Owner/agent + tenant.
RTB forms page ↗
RTB-46 + RTB-12L-DR

Direct Request (Unpaid Rent or Utilities)

The streamlined RTB path when rent stays unpaid after a 10-day notice: file the Direct Request application (RTB-12L-DR) together with the Landlord Direct Request Worksheet (RTB-46).

When to use
Unpaid rent/utilities after the RTB-30 notice window.
Who signs
Owner/agent.
RTB forms page ↗
RTB-12L series — official

Application for Dispute Resolution

Starts an RTB case to enforce your rights — deposits, damage claims, unpaid rent, or possession after a notice. Landlord forms: RTB-12L-CT (current tenancy), RTB-12L-PT (past tenancy), RTB-12L-EXH (expedited), RTB-12L-O (other).

When to use
When a tenant disputes a notice, or money/possession is owed.
Who signs
Owner/agent (landlord versions).

Category 7

Setup & Sharing

This portal is one self-contained HTML file (logos are embedded). The two service agreements are ready to use: download the pre-filled Word versions, fill in the party details, and sign. The .md files are editable text that copies cleanly into Word or Google Docs.

  1. Open it: double-click portal.html — it opens in any browser with no server and no internet needed for the page itself (fonts load from Google Fonts when online; offline fallbacks still display).
  2. Share a link: upload portal.html plus the .md files and the assets/ folder to any static host — Netlify Drop (app.netlify.com/drop), GitHub Pages, or the brokerage website. Keep all files in the same folder so the links work.
  3. Share as a package: zip the whole toolkit folder and send it; the recipient unzips and opens portal.html.
  4. Keep it current: replace the official-form links with fresh downloads whenever the RTB or BCFSA updates a form, and update the compliance foundation when rates and bylaws change.
  5. Industry check: see 04-industry-research.md for what competing BC property managers use (document sets, fees, service models) and the additions still missing from this kit.
