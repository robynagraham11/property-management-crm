## 1. Licensing under RESA and BCFSA — the single biggest gate

In British Columbia, managing or placing residential rental properties **for others, for a fee or other remuneration, is "real estate services"** under the *Real Estate Services Act* (RESA). You must be licensed by the **BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA)** — through a licensed brokerage — before you:

- advertise a rental,
- show a unit,
- screen tenants,
- negotiate or sign a tenancy agreement,
- collect rent or a security deposit, or
- accept any fee for any of the above.

**How it works (the short version):**

1. The **brokerage** — Stonehaus Realty Corp. — must hold a BCFSA licence that **authorizes rental property management services** (not every real estate brokerage is automatically authorized for this).
2. **You** (Robyn Graham) must be a licensee **under that brokerage**, licensed to provide rental property management services (a trading services licensee or rental property manager licensee engaged by a brokerage authorized for RPM).
3. **All fees must flow through the brokerage.** You may not be paid directly by owners or tenants for property management work. Your agreements are with the brokerage; you act on its behalf.

### ⚠️ Gap check — answer these today

| Question | Why it matters | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Does Stonehaus Realty Corp.'s BCFSA brokerage licence include **rental property management services**? | Without it, the brokerage (and you) are providing unlicensed real estate services. | Confirm in writing with your managing broker; verify with BCFSA at **bcfsa.ca** (licence search). |
| Are you licensed under the brokerage **for rental property management**? | A general trading services licence is not automatically an RPM authorization. | Confirm your licence status with your managing broker and BCFSA. |

**Consequences of operating unlicensed:** BCFSA discipline penalties can reach hundreds of thousands of dollars, plus cease-and-desist orders, inability to enforce your management agreements, and potential offences. **Do not operate on an assumption — get confirmation in writing.** *(Exact penalty amounts change; verify current BCFSA penalty schedules.)*

## 2. Business licence — City or Township of Langley

Two separate municipalities use the "Langley" name, and their rules differ. Confirm which one applies to you and to each rental property:

- **City of Langley** and **Township of Langley** both require **business licences** for businesses operating in their jurisdiction — including a property management/real estate business operated from a local address.
- **Both municipalities also require residential landlords to hold a residential rental business licence** for most long-term rental properties. As the manager you are not the licence holder, but you should confirm with each owner that their property is properly licensed, because operating an unlicensed rental can bring municipal fines and bylaw actions that disrupt the tenancy.

**To verify:** check the current business licence bylaws at **langleycity.ca** or **tol.ca** (Township of Langley). Confirm (a) your own business licence requirement, and (b) whether each managed property's owner holds the required residential rental business licence.

## 3. Trust account obligations (RESA + RTA)

Every dollar you touch on behalf of an owner or tenant is governed by trust rules:

1. **All trust money goes through the brokerage's BCFSA-authorized trust account** — rent collected, security deposits, pet damage deposits. Never through a personal account.
2. **Deposit promptly.** Money received for real estate services must be deposited into trust without delay (the brokerage's rules set the exact deadline — confirm with your managing broker).
3. **Never co-mingle.** Owners' rent and tenants' deposits sit in the brokerage pooled trust account, tracked to each property. No personal use, ever, not even temporarily.
4. **Security and pet damage deposits** are governed by the *Residential Tenancy Act* (RTA), s. 38: they must be returned within **15 days** of the tenant giving a forwarding address (unless the tenant agrees in writing to a deduction or the RTB orders otherwise), and **interest accrues at the rate prescribed each year** under the RTA Regulation and must be credited annually. *(Verify the current interest rate each year — see the RTB website.)*
5. **Monthly accounting** to owners: every month, a statement showing rents received, deposits held, disbursements, and fees. This is both best practice and essential to RESA record-keeping duties.

## 4. Record keeping

Under RESA and BCFSA rules, the brokerage must keep records of all trust transactions and trading records. In practice this means you must keep, for each property:

- owner agreements and signed agency disclosures;
- every tenancy agreement, addendum, notice, inspection report, and correspondence;
- receipts and statements for all money received and paid;
- advertising and screening records.

**Retention is generally at least seven years** — confirm the current requirement with your managing broker, and keep everything in the brokerage's approved filing/software system from day one.

## 5. Insurance

| Coverage | Required or recommended | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Errors & omissions (E&O)** | **Mandatory** — brokerages in BC must maintain E&O through the Real Estate Errors and Omissions Insurance Corporation program | Confirm the brokerage's E&O covers **rental property management** activities, not only trading services. |
| **General liability** (brokerage) | Required in practice | Confirm coverage extends to property management work. |
| **Fidelity / theft coverage** for trust monies | Strongly recommended | Ask your managing broker what the brokerage carries. |
| **Owner's property and liability insurance** | You must require it in every management agreement | The owner insures the building and liability; you are not the owner's insurer. Add the brokerage as an additional insured where the insurer allows. |
| **Tenant insurance** | Recommend in writing | You can require it in the tenancy agreement as a lawful additional term; keep proof on file. |

## 6. Other things to have in place before day one

- **Agency disclosure (RESA):** before providing services, you must give owners and tenants the BCFSA-prescribed **"Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services — Rental Property Management"** forms. See `03-disclosures-and-statutory-forms.md`. (Verify current BCFSA form titles/versions.)
- **Privacy (BC PIPA):** tenant applications contain personal information. Collect only what is needed, get consent, store securely, and dispose of it properly. Have the brokerage's privacy policy handy.
- **GST:** management and placement fees attract **5% GST**. Residential rent itself is GST-exempt. Invoices for fees must be issued by the brokerage.
- **Strata properties:** if you manage a strata lot, you need the strata's **bylaws and rules**, and you must give tenants **Form K — Notice of Tenant's Responsibilities** (Strata Property Act). Never assume a strata allows rentals, pets, or your signage — check the bylaws first.
- **Trust but verify:** this page summarizes the rules. The *Residential Tenancy Act*, *Real Estate Services Act*, BCFSA Real Estate Rules, and municipal bylaws change. Confirm current versions before relying on anything.
